# The Built-in Terminal

The terminal is a place where you can type commands and see the results without leaving Lumina. You do not need to be a programmer to use it — it is simply another tool, like a calculator for your computer.

---

## Opening and closing the terminal

Press `Ctrl + J` to open the terminal below your note. Press it again to hide it.

You can also use the close button in the terminal header when you are finished with a session.

---

## What you can do with it

- **Run commands** on your own computer.
- **Keep several terminals open** at once, each in its own session.
- **Switch between sessions** without losing your place.
- **Rename a session** so you remember what it is for.
- **See at a glance** whether a session is running or has stopped.
  - A green dot means it is running.
  - A red dot means it has stopped.

---

## Choosing your shell

A "shell" is the program that understands your commands. Lumina finds the shells already installed on your computer, so you do not have to set anything up.

**On Windows**, you can choose from:

- Git Bash
- PowerShell
- PowerShell 7, if it is installed
- Command Prompt
- Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL), if you have it ready

**On macOS and Linux**, Lumina looks for Zsh, Bash, Fish, and the standard system shell.

Open the shell menu in the terminal header and pick the one you prefer.

---

## Making the terminal comfortable

- **Resize it:** drag the top edge to make the terminal taller or shorter.
- **Resize the session list:** drag the left edge to widen or narrow it.
- **Reset the session list width:** double-click the divider.
- **Maximize:** give the terminal the whole workspace. Your note tabs stay visible at the top.
- **Minimize:** click the same button again to bring your note back.

---

## Good to know

- Commands run on your computer, so they can change files. Only run commands you understand or trust.
- Closing a session ends whatever is running inside it.
- When you quit Lumina, it closes any terminals that are still open, so nothing is left running in the background.

---

> **Tip:** If you are new to terminals, open one and explore it gently. Your notes live in their own tabs and stay safe while you look around.
