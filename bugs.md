Honestly, Saboor, the boundaries in Lumina are less about what the app can't do and more about what it deliberately chooses not to do, and those choices are actually the whole point of the design. The biggest one is that Lumina will never lock your notes inside a proprietary database or force them into a cloud you don't control. Everything lives as plain Markdown files on your own disk, which means there's no hidden format, no vendor holding your thoughts hostage, and no requirement to be online. If you wanted to, you could open every single note in VS Code or Obsidian and Lumina would simply notice the change and sync up. That's a freedom most note apps quietly take away from you.

The second thing it can't do is reach outside your workspace. I only see the files and folders inside this specific Lumina vault, so I can't browse your entire Documents folder, peek at other drives, or touch anything on your system beyond what you've placed here. That's a privacy guarantee, not a limitation I'm apologizing for. Along the same lines, Lumina doesn't run in the cloud by default, so there's no always-on server syncing your notes behind your back unless you explicitly set up something like the Google Drive mirroring feature yourself.

There are also a few practical constraints worth knowing. It won't magically understand a note you haven't given it context for, since the AI is grounded in your actual workspace rather than some omniscient external knowledge. It also won't rewrite your entire document when you ask for a small edit, because surgical updates are baked in to protect your frontmatter and surrounding sections. And it won't commit or push anything to Git unless you explicitly ask, which keeps your version history firmly under your control. So really, the things Lumina "can't" do are mostly the things that would compromise your ownership, privacy, or control, and that's a trade I think you'll appreciate.
